\note{%
\textbf{Message.} The jury is from ExxonMobil. Their Outlook frames the challenge as ``more energy \emph{and} lower emissions'' and says that ``no single pathway can be reasonably predicted''. We answer both halves for India's electricity: a probability distribution instead of one path, and a plan tested against it.\par\medskip
\textbf{Evidence.} Our side: median 4,688\,TWh in FY2047-48 vs 1,698\,TWh in FY2023-24 (2.76$\times$, +176\%); robust plan 891\,Mt vs base plan 1,367\,Mt CO$_2$ (CEA v22 factors: coal 0.971, gas 0.470\,t/MWh); robust coal share 19.0\% (\texttt{presets.csv}, run of 22 Nov 2026). ExxonMobil's side: India's energy demand rises from 46.6 to 77.6 quadrillion BTU (2025--2050) while world demand rises by 73.3; 31.0/73.3 $\approx$ 42\%. This is our arithmetic on their data, not their wording; say so if asked.\par\medskip
\textbf{Likely question.} \qa{Why compare electricity growth with energy growth?}{We do not equate them: the 65\% is ExxonMobil's world electricity figure; India's 176\% is utility generation over a shorter horizon from a lower base.}
