# Assumptions, uncertainties and limitations

Every assumption below is either estimated from data, taken from a cited source, or exposed as a dashboard control.
The "Control" column names the dashboard slider where one exists.

| --- | --- |
| Population | Draw-level normal shift scaled to the UN low/high band |
| Urbanisation | Draw-level normal shift growing to 2 points by 2047 |
| Income growth | Persistent shift plus AR(1) annual shocks |
| Model structure | Primary: one driver model; the no-urban-term and damped-trend slots are ablations. Cross-country check: three models mixed by backtest weights |
| Model error | Primary: AR(1) residual of ln(kWh per person), starting at the FY2023-24 residual (+0.072), persistence 0.51, innovation s.d. 0.032 |
| Parameter estimates | Primary: 2,000 moving-block (3-year) bootstrap replicates of the residuals of the years without the pandemic dummy, rescaled for degrees of freedom, half from each estimation window, each with a gamma draw from its prior (truncated normal). | State convergence | Normal draw of the convergence speed (mean 0.0021, s.d. 0.0051) |
| Demand features | 0 in the headline. ## 5. Limitations

1. **Regional requirement is modelled, not observed.** Under the generation-only rule there is no regional need series to validate against. The base-year split is checked for plausibility against regional generation shares (which include transfers). The challenge dataset has no state urban share, so states that urbanise faster than India get no extra weight. NPP's five grid regions and the dataset's six zones do not match; the split follows NPP, which the organisers accepted: "Four, five, or six regions - either is fine" ([decisions.md](decisions.md), D12). A state panel on NPP state generation cannot validate the split either, because it records where plants stand (`outputs/state_panel_check.csv`; its standard error is not clustered by state).
2. **Industrial structure.** Heavy industry (steel, aluminium, cement) is concentrated in the Eastern and Western regions. Population and income alone understate it. A sector-based extension is future work.
3. **Annual energy in the plan.** The plan balances annual energy. Storage and the extra generation that hourly balancing needs are worked out afterwards from one representative year ([results.md](results.md), section 3.6) and do not feed back into the plan. Ramping and reserve margins are not modelled; firm-generation floors are a proxy for reliability.
4. **Eighteen annual points.** India's annual NPP series starts in FY2006-07, so only the income elasticity is estimated from it. A quadratic, a saturation curve or a free urban coefficient cannot be identified. The uncertainty of the forecast is driven mostly by the income path, not by the model form.
5. **Supply-constrained history.** Energy shortages closed between the late 2000s and FY2017-18, so part of past generation growth was catch-up, which can raise the estimated elasticity. The FY2011-12 window and the first-difference estimates (0.786 and 0.789) are lower than the full-window estimate (0.872); the elasticity-decline setting shows the size of the effect.
6. **Costs to 2047 are uncertain.** IEA projections are a single published path. The sensitivity sweep varies solar, coal and nuclear costs by plus or minus 30 percent, and the CEA cost basis re-solves the recommended plan with Indian cost inputs, in real 2024 USD like the IEA basis (202.6 against 176.2 billion USD a year at 4 percent inflation). Asked whether published potentials, costs and emission factors may serve as limits and prices inside the mix optimisation, the organisers replied "Use insights from this data source". That answer is not explicit; a clarification has been requested and no reply has come yet ([decisions.md](decisions.md), D12). If the organisers rule that published figures may only justify the mix, the 2047 requirement and its regional split do not change.
7. **Captive generation.** The forecast covers NPP utility generation only. Captive plants inside industries are outside it, and under the organisers' ruling they are neither estimated nor planned.
8. **Model-error persistence from a short series.** The persistence of the residual (0.51) comes from the Durbin-Watson statistic of 13 annual residuals, not from a direct lag regression, which the slow drift of the early, supply-constrained years would dominate.
9. **Placeholder income data.** Five states and union territories repeat a GSDP figure across years in the organiser data (four of them from 2006 to 2010, Nagaland in 2007). Those year pairs are left out of national income growth, so national income growth for 2007 to 2010 rests on the remaining states. In the convergence estimate each state's base year skips its placeholder run, but:
   - Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep also repeat their 2023 value in 2024, so their 2024 relative income, the end point of the estimate and the start of the split, rests on a placeholder;
   - the national GSDP per person that relative incomes are measured against still includes the 2006-2010 placeholder values;
   - the convergence speed depends on how placeholders are read: 0.0021 as used, 0.0166 under the literal reading of the rule, -0.0005 without Delhi and Chandigarh ([decisions.md](decisions.md), D14).
10. **No feasible plan at very low requirements.** The coal floor and the gas kept for peaking are fixed in TWh, while the non-fossil minimum is a share of generation. The Robust policy therefore has no feasible plan below about (647 + 51) / 0.25 = 2,794 TWh of generation (2,780 TWh found by bisection), which includes the Low growth scenario's P05 requirement (2,306 TWh). The Base (about 2,590 TWh), Energy security (about 3,020 TWh) and Nuclear mission policies are also infeasible there; Low carbon is feasible. Every policy is feasible at the Base scenario's P05 (3,190 TWh) and at every scenario's median (`outputs/presets.csv`; [decisions.md](decisions.md), D13). High growth with the Energy security policy has no feasible plan at P90.
11. **Regional renewables are secondary.** NPP has no renewable generation by region or state, so the FY2023-24 base mix takes them from the CEA General Review. Its total (1,734 TWh) is 36 TWh above NPP's national 1,698 TWh, because CEA's renewables total is above NPP's.
12. **Published limits and floors are approximations.** The existing-fleet floors are best-year maxima (hydro in the wet FY2025-26, gas in FY2020-21). FY2024-25 and FY2025-26 have no published build-limit rows and take the first period's rate. Published coal additions are gross of retirements (the 110 percent coal ceiling binds first). The committed nuclear and hydro floors ignore commissioning dates, and the hydro CUF (0.36) is the lowest published value. The Robust policy's gas is fully sited by the FY2023-24 gas shares.
13. **Evidence on the central path.** The growth decomposition splits the central path (4,589 TWh), not the median (4,688 TWh).
14. **One state's GSDP at current prices.** Jammu and Kashmir's GSDP from 2012 equals MoSPI current prices and is deflated with the national GDP deflator, not a state deflator: the deflated values lie -2.0 to +4.2 percent from MoSPI's constant-price values over 2012-2021. Its 2022-2024 values are predicted in the dataset and lie +7.9, +5.8 and -3.5 percent away. The price basis of its 2006-2010 placeholder value was not checked.
15. **The demand-feature layer** ([demand_features.md](demand_features.md)).
    - T&D losses are an upper bound: their past fall is inside the elasticity.
    - New loads are grossed up at FY2023-24 loss rates, so with T&D losses active they are overstated by up to about 10 percent.
    - Storage round-trip losses must not be added on top of the plan's own storage losses.
    - Distributed renewables under the RCO overlap rooftop solar and PM-KUSUM.
    - Whether feeder-level solar (PM-KUSUM C) is inside NPP utility generation is not stated; if it is, that row's effect is zero.
    - The data-centre high is our extrapolation of an official 2031-32 figure, not an official 2047 figure: it continues the Ministry of Power's 26.3 GW of AI data-centre load in 2031-32 linearly to 2047 and reads it as average load (x 8.76 = 230.4 TWh); read as capacity at 50 percent utilisation it would add nothing above the central (ruling R30).
    - The imports high assumes the full 10,000 MW Nepal target, which has no schedule or contract; the storage high reads CEA's 2047 energy storage as batteries cycled once a day (ruling R35).
    - Factors from official conferences and texts are context only: none gives an India figure in TWh beyond the trend (ruling R36).
    - Telecom networks, consumer electronics, semiconductor manufacturing and AI compute have no official electricity projection and are context rows.
    - The unit and year-conversion factors are official but fixed, and the trend rule uses the multiple of the 20 Nov headline (x2.7697; x2.7607 now, under 2 TWh for any row).
    - Rows are summed and drawn independently, and features are split across regions by each region's share of the requirement, because no official correlations or regional split exist.
16. **Price base proxies.** FY2024-25 stands for 2024 and is paired with the calendar-2024 exchange rate. The FY2026-27 deflator (biomass fuel) is extended at the RBI target and is not official. Hydro, bioenergy and the firmed-RE premium are tariff ratios from different years (2020 to 2026-27), treated as inflation-neutral, which a ratio across years is not strictly. The nominal 2047 outlook carries the RBI target past FY2030-31, the last year notified.
17. **The Nuclear mission policy** ([results.md](results.md), section 3.9; [decisions.md](decisions.md), D15). It is a policy option, not the recommendation. Its floor is for 2047 only and ignores the road map's commissioning path; it runs nuclear at CUF 0.68 (the CEA NEP availability), so it may understate the mission's energy by up to 95.2 TWh if the fleet reaches the road map's 80 percent; the captive small reactors are left out by the organisers' ruling; its siting follows the model's reactor key, not the road map's 52,540 MW (313.0 TWh at CUF 0.68) without an identified site; nuclear's higher auxiliary use (about 28 TWh more for the 406.5 TWh moved) is not in the annual balance; and its storage need was not back-calculated.

The growth presets are shifts relative to each core's own historical growth, so they apply to both cores.
They also move urbanisation, because faster income growth comes with faster urbanisation.
Any demand-feature scenario can be applied on top of any growth preset, and any policy preset solves the resulting requirement ([demand_features.md](demand_features.md), section 6).
