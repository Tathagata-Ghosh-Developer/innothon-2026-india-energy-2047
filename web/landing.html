What drives the uncertainty

Income growth. If income per person grows 1.5 points a year slower or faster than its history, the {{years.target_label}} median moves by {{sensitivity.growth_minus_1_5.change_pct|0}}% or {{sensitivity.growth_plus_1_5.change_pct|+0}}%.

How it works

  1. Inside the model, electricity generated per person is linked to income per person using India's own data: from {{elasticity.from}} to {{elasticity.to}}, each 1% rise in income came with about {{elasticity.beta|2}}% more generation per person.
  2. {{scenarios|0}} scenarios of income growth, population, urbanisation and that link give the country's total in TWh for {{years.target_label}}, split by region from each state's population and income in the challenge dataset.
  3. A least-cost planning model then chooses what to build in each region within land, build-rate, policy and transmission limits, and an hour-by-hour check sizes storage and land.

Data rules

The core 2047 prediction uses only the two challenge datasets: generation from the National Power Portal (NPP) and the organisers' state population and GSDP.

Only energy generated (TWh) is forecast and compared; installed capacity and energy consumption are never inputs. Published studies supply only planning limits such as costs, resource potential, build rates and emissions.

A cross-country model of 153 countries gives {{national.cross_country_p50_twh|0}} TWh for {{national.cross_country_year}} (calendar year, total generation beyond NPP's utility series); it is an independent check used for justification, not for estimation. Years are fiscal years: {{years.target_label}} runs from April {{years.target}} to March of the next year.
