Drivers

What else drives 2047

Income, population and urbanisation drive the forecast, but much else can change what India must generate. We listed every driver we found, {{features.counts.total|0}} in all, each with its source, and sized in TWh those that official figures allow.

What this layer is, and what it is not

  • The headline stays the two-dataset estimate. The {{national.p50|0}} TWh for {{years.target_label}} is estimated on NPP and the Renewable India dataset alone. Nothing in this section changes it.
  • The features are an official-source layer on top. Each quantified driver is a signed TWh adjustment from official sources: ministries, statutory bodies such as CEA and BEE, and NITI Aayog (secondary, labelled). Only its part beyond the historical trend is counted, phased in linearly from {{years.base_label}} and shared across the regions in proportion to their requirement.
  • T&D losses are an upper bound. Lower grid losses would cut the generation needed, but that gain may already be inside the historical trend. It counts as 0 at central, at its upper bound only in the two downside cases (Efficiency and distributed, All downside), and as a draw between 0 and that bound in the joint scenarios.

{{features.counts.total|0}} drivers, by how the model treats them

  • {{features.counts.core|0}}EstimatedInside the two-dataset estimate, such as population, income and the efficiency programmes already in the historical trend.
  • {{features.counts.driver|0}}Model driversCarried by the forecast as an assumption, a setting or its model error, such as the urbanisation effect.
  • {{features.counts.layer|0}}Quantified layerSized in TWh for {{years.target_label}} from official figures. These make the scenarios below.
  • {{features.counts.excluded_rule|0}}Excluded by ruleOutside the forecast's scope by the organisers' ruling, such as captive generation, which NPP's utility series leaves out.
  • {{features.counts.context|0}}ContextListed with its source but not added: no official figure separates it from the trend, it is already inside it, or it is a target rather than a load.

The joint distribution: every feature at once

{{features.joint.p50_twh|0}}TWh

Median of Core plus every quantified feature drawn at random over its official range: {{features.joint.change_p50_pct|+0}}% on the headline, with 90% of scenarios between {{features.joint.p05_twh|0}} and {{features.joint.p95_twh|0}} TWh. The features alone add {{features.joint.features_p50_twh|0}} TWh at the median (90%: {{features.joint.features_p05_twh|0}} to {{features.joint.features_p95_twh|0}} TWh).

Each feature is drawn on its own, triangular between its low, central and high values; T&D losses are drawn evenly between 0 and their upper bound. Every combination of feature levels lies between All downside ({{features.scenarios.all_downside.adjustment_twh|+0}} TWh) and All upside ({{features.scenarios.all_upside.adjustment_twh|+0}} TWh) on top of Core.

Evidence

Forecasting what India must generate, and how to meet it, by 2047

In our words: a data-driven forecast of India's total generation requirement in 2047, and a realistic regional plan for meeting it across energy sources.

The model projects national and regional needs from population, income and urbanisation; the plan then recommends a generation mix for each region that reflects local strengths, practical limits and a defensible path. Assumptions, methods and uncertainty are stated with each result.

Forecast the requirement. Match regional strengths. Explain the mix.

Key evidence sources

Primary: the two challenge datasets, the National Power Portal and the Renewable India dataset. Every other source is secondary and labelled. Three outside inputs shape the 2047 requirement: the UN population and urban-share paths, used as driver assumptions as the organisers ruled and checked against the dataset's state growth, and the size of the urbanisation effect, our judgment from published studies. The MoSPI GDP deflator corrects one input: Jammu and Kashmir's GSDP, which the dataset gives at current prices from FY2012-13, is put on constant prices like every other state's. All other secondary sources set only limits and prices in the mix, or size the demand features, which stay outside the headline.

  • Historical energy generation

    Past trends behind the quantitative forecast.

    • PrimaryNPP national generation, {{national.history.0.label}} to {{years.base_label}}; regional generation by source; hourly generation by source

  • Population and demographics

    Population growth and urbanisation paths.

    • PrimaryRenewable India dataset: state population
    • SecondaryUN World Population Prospects 2024: the population path of the forecast, checked against the dataset's state growth. UN World Urbanization Prospects 2018: the urban-share path. Both shape the requirement.

  • Generation mix

    Today's baseload and renewable generation in each region.

    • PrimaryNPP coal, gas, nuclear and hydro generation by region, {{years.base_label}}
    • SecondaryCEA state-wise renewable generation; costs (IEA World Energy Outlook 2025, CEA), all in real 2024 USD through MoSPI, RBI and US BEA price indices; build limits (CEA National Electricity Plan), CO₂ factors (CEA CO₂ Baseline Database)

  • Geographic and climatic indicators

    Sunshine, wind and water, region by region.

    • PrimaryNPP hourly solar, wind and hydro profiles for the storage check
    • SecondaryMNRE and NISE solar potential, NIWE wind potential at 150 m, CEA hydro reassessment, MNRE offshore wind zones

  • Land availability

    Land suitable for utility-scale solar and wind.

    • SecondaryWastelands Atlas of India, forest cover (ISFR 2023), MNRE land norm and published land-use intensity of solar and wind

  • Economic development and urbanisation

    Income growth by state, and towns and cities growing faster than income alone implies.

    • PrimaryRenewable India dataset: state GSDP at constant prices
    • SecondaryMoSPI GDP deflator: Jammu and Kashmir's GSDP, given at current prices from FY2012-13, deflated to constant prices
    • SecondarySize of the urbanisation effect, from published studies (our judgment; the run without it is always shown)

Method

From a transparent forecast to a regional plan you can explore

  1. Step 01

    Forecast

    National and region-wise requirement

    Generation per person follows income per person, with India's own elasticity of {{elasticity.beta|2}}, and urbanisation; {{scenarios|0}} scenarios of income, population, urbanisation and that link give the {{years.target_label}} range, split into the {{len regions.list}} grid regions from each state's population and income.

    Key principle: every assumption is shown with what changing it does. Without our urbanisation effect the forecast gives {{sensitivity.no_urban_term.p50_twh|0}} TWh ({{sensitivity.no_urban_term.change_pct|+0}}%).

  3. Step 02

    Strategy

    Generation mix by region

    A least-cost plan picks each region's mix within usable land, published build limits, a {{plan.settings.nonfossil_min_pct|0}}% non-fossil floor and the corridors between regions; an hour-by-hour check then sizes storage. It covers the core energy sources:

    • Coal
    • Hydro
    • Solar
    • Wind

Data rules

The 2047 prediction is estimated on the two challenge datasets: generation from the National Power Portal (NPP) and the organisers' state population and GSDP. Its population and urban-share paths come from the UN (World Population Prospects 2024, World Urbanization Prospects 2018; secondary), as driver assumptions the organisers' ruling allows, checked against the dataset's state growth; the size of the urbanisation effect is our judgment from published studies, and the run without it is always shown.

Only energy generated (TWh) is forecast and compared; installed capacity and energy consumption are never inputs to the forecast. All other published sources supply only limits and prices for the mix (costs, resource potential, build rates and emissions) or size the demand features, an official-source layer kept outside the headline. Every cost is in real 2024 USD.

Years are fiscal years: {{years.target_label}} runs from April {{years.target}} to March of the next year.

FAQ

The midpoint panel's questions, answered

The five questions the panel asked on 18 November, with short answers from the current results.

01Is income growth the only factor behind the 2047 requirement?

No. The requirement is population times generation per person. Generation per person follows income per person (elasticity {{elasticity.beta|2}}) and urbanisation beyond what income already implies, and the model carries its own error, which fades over time. Of the growth to {{years.target_label}}, income accounts for {{growth_decomposition.income_pct|0}}%, population for {{growth_decomposition.population_pct|0}}%, urbanisation for {{growth_decomposition.urbanisation_pct|0}}% and the model's error for {{growth_decomposition.model_error_pct|0}}%.

  • Income growth 1.5 points a year higher or lower: {{sensitivity.growth_plus_1_5.change_pct|+0}}% or {{sensitivity.growth_minus_1_5.change_pct|0}}%.
  • Urban share 5 points above the UN path: {{sensitivity.urban_plus_5.change_pct|+0}}%; no urbanisation effect: {{sensitivity.no_urban_term.change_pct|0}}%.
  • Population range twice as wide: {{sensitivity.pop_band_x2.change_pct|+1}}%.
  • Demand features beyond the historical trend, such as transport electrification, green hydrogen and data centres, less rooftop solar and other offsets, from official sources (secondary), are a separate layer outside the headline: a median of {{features.scenarios.features_central.p50_twh|0}} TWh ({{features.scenarios.features_central.change_pct|+0}}%) with every feature at its central value, and {{features.joint.p50_twh|0}} TWh ({{features.joint.change_p50_pct|+0}}%) with the features drawn jointly. See what else drives 2047.
02Is income data used state-wise, region-wise or for India as a whole?

State-wise. Each of the {{regions.state_count|0}} states and union territories in the Renewable India dataset has its own income per person, from its GSDP at constant prices and its population. Jammu and Kashmir's GSDP, which the dataset gives at current prices from FY2012-13, is first deflated with the MoSPI GDP deflator; leaving the state out of national income growth instead moves the {{years.target_label}} median by {{sensitivity.jk_excluded.change_pct|+1}}%.

Relative incomes move towards the national average at a convergence rate estimated from the same data, each state measured from its first year that is not a repeated placeholder value. The dashboard shows the estimated rate.

Each state's population grows at India's UN path plus its own recent trend in the dataset, and its generation per person follows its own income; states are weighted by population and summed into NPP's {{len regions.list}} grid regions.

03Renewable energy data is missing or mostly empty. How did you manage?
  • The forecast needs only total generation, which NPP publishes up to {{years.base_label}}.
  • For the two held-back test years NPP has conventional but no renewable generation, so renewable output is extended at its recent growth rate, and that estimate is labelled.
  • For today's regional mix, NPP gives each region's coal, gas, nuclear and hydro generation (primary); CEA's state-wise renewable generation (secondary, labelled) fills only the gap NPP leaves.
  • Where solar and wind are being built (Renewable India dataset) is a qualitative check on regional strengths only; installed capacity never enters a calculation.
04How does the elasticity affect the result, and at what level was it estimated?

For India as a whole: NPP generation per person against income per person, {{elasticity.from}} to {{elasticity.to}}, gives {{elasticity.beta|2}}, so each 1% more income per person has come with about {{elasticity.beta|2}}% more generation per person. The regional split applies the same national income response to each state's own income.

  • One standard error lower or higher: {{sensitivity.beta_minus_1se.change_pct|+1}}% or {{sensitivity.beta_plus_1se.change_pct|+1}}% in {{years.target_label}}.
  • An elasticity that falls as incomes rise: {{sensitivity.elasticity_decline_0_2.change_pct|+0}}%; the first-difference estimate: {{sensitivity.beta_first_difference.change_pct|+1}}%.

A state-level regression is not used for the forecast: power plants sit near coal and water, not where the power is used, so a state's own generation says little about its demand.

05What hurdles did you face, and how are you dealing with them?
  • Rules on outside data. We asked the organisers in writing and applied their ruling: the requirement is estimated on NPP and the Renewable India dataset, with the UN population and urban-share paths as stated driver assumptions (secondary, checked against the dataset) and an urbanisation effect sized from published studies; the MoSPI GDP deflator puts Jammu and Kashmir's current-price GSDP on constant prices; all other published sources set only limits and prices in the mix, or size the demand features shown outside the headline. Without our urbanisation effect the forecast gives {{sensitivity.no_urban_term.p50_twh|0}} TWh.
  • Wide uncertainty. Income growth dominates, so the plan is also tested at the P90 requirement ({{plan.p90.requirement_twh|0}} TWh): the Robust policy leaves {{plan.p90.robust_unmet_twh|0}} TWh unmet, and making {{plan.p90.levers_solar_land_pct|0}}% of the solar potential usable brings that to {{plan.p90.levers_unmet_twh|0}} TWh.
  • Missing regional renewable data. Handled as in question 3, with every secondary source labelled.
  • Evening peaks an annual plan can hide. The plan is run hour by hour through a year of NPP hourly generation; it needs {{storage.energy_gwh|0}} GWh of storage, about {{storage.duration_h|1}} hours.
